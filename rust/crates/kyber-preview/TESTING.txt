KYBER — FIRST NATIVE WINDOWS CURSOR TEST

This is a Windows 11 x64 test app. It replaces the pointer only inside its own
window. It is not the desktop-wide Kyber app yet. No installation is needed.

1. Extract the entire ZIP to a folder on your Windows PC.
2. Open kyber-preview.exe. Keep the frames folder beside it.
3. Move the pointer over the white and dark areas. Hold the LEFT mouse button.
4. Move slowly, then quickly, and cross the white/dark boundary while holding.
5. Release, then press again several times. Also try quick taps.
6. Hold and move outside the window. Your normal pointer should return, with
   no leftover glow. Move back in and make a fresh press to ignite again.
7. Try switching to another app while holding. Return and try another press.

Keys:
  Space / Right / Left — change color
  B — split, white, or dark background
  V — matching, fixed light, or fixed dark glow variant
  S — all sounds, power-up only, or mute
  Escape — close

Please report: does the glow stay attached to the blade during movement?
Does it keep the appearance approved in Chrome at 100%? Do ignition, release,
repeated clicks, leaving the window, and switching apps behave properly?

Start with your usual 100% Windows display scaling. Other display scales and
moving between monitors are useful follow-up tests. The current trial limits
cursor images to 256 physical pixels and shows an error above that size.
Kyber includes the shared private recording pack for power-up, held hum and
power-down. No separate audio download or setup is needed. To override a role,
put WAV, MP3, OGG, OGA, or FLAC files in %APPDATA%\kyber-cursor\sounds\ignite,
sounds\off, or sounds\hum. The held recording loops with a short crossfade.
All plays the complete sequence; power-up-only omits the hold and release.
See AUDIO-NOTICE.md and sound-pack.json for recording provenance.
The Windows default audio device is used.
Move while holding the left button to draw a fading trail. It clears when
leaving the window or switching apps.

This is an experimental test build. Its build-info.json records how it was
built and checked. Real Windows interaction, movement, and audio playback still
need testing.
It is unsigned; signing/distribution are separate work before a public release.
